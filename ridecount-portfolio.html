Why I'm building this

The idea came
from my own stop.

Boarding pass — Origin story

Every day on the way to college, I watch the same thing happen: a bus pulls in already packed past capacity, and people climb on anyway — because no one knows if the next one will be any better, or when it'll even arrive. Elderly passengers get pushed to the back. People miss classes standing at the stop, or risk their safety squeezing into the doorway. Drivers and conductors have no reason to turn anyone away — more passengers is more revenue for them, every time. I didn't set out to build a startup. I set out to solve the one commute problem I complain about more than any other — and realised the fix wasn't stricter rules, it was better information, delivered before people ever reach the door.

How it works

Three steps.
No hardware.
No permissions.

Riders report it

Anyone on or near a bus taps one of four crowding levels — Empty, Comfortable, Crowded, Overcrowded. Takes under five seconds.

Waiting riders see it

People at the next stop see the latest reported status and an estimated arrival time, so they can choose to wait for a better option — an informed choice, not a forced one.

Patterns get surfaced

Reports build into a route-by-route, hour-by-hour picture of where overcrowding actually happens — the evidence transport planners need but don't currently have.

What's next

The route ahead.

Every stop on this line is a real milestone — not a feature wishlist. Here's exactly where RideCount is now, and where it's headed.

Current stop
MVP — crowd reporting + ETA

Rider-reported occupancy and live arrival estimates. Buildable and testable with zero government or operator approval.

Next stop
Pilot on 1–2 routes

Real usage data from my own college route and a nearby high-traffic route, to prove the reporting model actually predicts crowding.

Upcoming
GPS-verified live tracking

Driver-side location sharing replaces guesswork ETAs with real bus positions, tightening accuracy of arrival predictions.

Upcoming
Authority dashboard

Aggregated route-level data offered to RTC/RTO planners — the evidence base for adding trips where demand is chronically underserved.

Long-term
Sensor-verified & enforcement-linked capacity

Hardware occupancy sensors and ticketing integration, explored jointly with transport authorities once the data case is proven.
